## C-2026-11-A · Peak (owner call) · 18 Nov · S-rank

**Crucible:** a first B2B call with a studio owner who had been treating the founder as a prospective client.

**Moment of maximum friction:** "What do you offer?" The founder listed many things and said nothing.

**Rationalisations (deconstructed):**
- "Listen first, so don't lead with an offer." Listening first is right. Arriving without an offer is not. Diagnose them, but bring your own diagnosis too.
- "It's a yes." It was a yes to another meeting, inside a role the owner defined himself ("follow me for a few days" = a staff try-out).
- "He didn't get the memo." The memo was never one sentence long.

**Blind spot:** the founder went in to diagnose the owner's business without having diagnosed their own offer. They were a client (form filled, quote requested), a possible instructor, a possible team member and a possible partner, all at once. With the role left unnamed, the owner defaulted to the role he already has plenty of: a multi-job team member.

**Signal (what he gave us):**
- Leads and coaching hours dropped after the move from his old location, and he had to let trainers go.
- His small team covers several jobs (programmes, video, content). Next up: a podcast, a corporate campaign and Google Ads.
- His model is a holistic programme on three pillars (sleep, stress and mental health; nutrition; screening, mobility and strength). Pilates is new to them, and his Pilates instructor is unreliable.
- His future picture: Pilates for core stability inside the package, plus Pilates classes for fun, open to non-members too.
- The gap: his movement pillar has no owner for Pilates. That is the exact gap we fill.

**What worked:** asking permission to pivot after listening first. Closing with "what was most memorable to you?" got the owner to name the collaboration himself.

**The hard truth:** whoever names the role first owns the relationship. Leave yours unnamed and the buyer names the cheapest one they already have.

**The fix (filed as an update, no new ID):** Single-Point Anchor from the Architect's Cadence Protocol, now step 0 of Call card 2 (owner).
- Script: "I'd be the Pilates lead for your movement pillar. Not staff, not a client: a partner who owns that piece. A 90-day block for 3 to 5 clients: I design it, run it and track every client, then we decide."
- Trigger: they ask "what do you offer?" or "follow me for a few days".
- Action: say the anchor, then stop. Turn the shadowing into a 2-day diagnostic with a written output (D-009).
- Outcome: the conversation moves to scope and a date, not job tasks.

**Gaps and next step:**
- Before the next meeting: a one-page outline of where Pilates sits in his three pillars (the D-009 clarity product).
- Work out his monthly cost from the price list he sent (Q2, still open).

**Doctrine at play:** P-041 Doctor Frame (the diagnosis went well) · D-015 Architect Frame (lost at the offer) · D-009 Clarity before Momentum · SYS-P058 v2.1 Listen step.

**Public lesson (no names):** "I spent a whole call understanding a studio owner's business, then froze when he asked what I offer. If you don't name your role, they'll name it for you."

## C-2026-11-C · Friday visit run (MOVE · Movement Dynamics · TRIBE) · 20 Nov

**Crucible:** three studio visits in one day. One real sit-down (MOVE, the branch manager); two contact-gathering stops.

**Moment of maximum friction (MOVE):** leading with the system, then realising mid-conversation it wasn't the path. They already have an in-house website and a trainer app, after leaving a third-party build over price. The founder then pivoted to the recovery station, i.e. a second offer in one meeting, and kept switching words ("nutrition cafe", "metabolic recovery station").

**What it taught us about our own system:**
- The HVO rule already says "fuel first, system second". The system was the weaker alternative here (BATNA = your best fallback if this deal doesn't happen; ZOPA = the range where both sides can agree). Check before you pitch: do they already have a website or app? If yes, lead with fuel.
- One name, one sentence: "RECUP.STN, a recovery station at the Health Bar in DC Mall." Not "nutrition cafe", not "metabolic". Fewer words, the same words every time.
- The daily-station model ("pick your busiest class") doesn't fit private 1-on-1 studios with uneven traffic. For them the way in is **events** (Vimal's own advice: exposure, then trust).

**What worked:** daring to ask and daring to follow up. Direct and raw got a yes to a Health Bar visit. Asking "which part could I have explained more clearly?" got a real answer ("the nutrition part made it clearer").

**Blind spot:** no proof to show. No photos, no success story, no numbers on paper. A values-first gatekeeper (who turns down big brands that don't fit) needs to see who we are before any offer.

**Weak close:** "What was most memorable?" got "just you showing up". Replace it with a close that moves things forward: **"What would you need to see to bring this to your marketing director?"**

**Contact-gathering stops (Movement Dynamics, TRIBE):** get the name, get the number, get out. First principles: front-line staff don't want control, they want clarity. Both suggested WhatsApp + an Instagram DM together, so the founders see it.

**The hard truth:** trust comes before the offer, and trust needs something they can see.

**The fix (updates, no new IDs):**
1. A proof pack (photos, one sourced number, one member story, values in 3 lines). It's on the calendar for Mon 23 Nov.
2. Owner call card: replace "most memorable" with the decision-maker question.
3. Studio triage before pitching: own website or app? → fuel only. Private 1-on-1 studio? → events, not the daily station.

**Public lesson (no names):** "I pitched software to a studio that had just built its own. Ten minutes in, I knew. Now I ask before I pitch."

## C-2026-11-D · Event partner: "very AI generated" (25 Nov)
**What happened:** the event pack showed the venue, not the drinks, in a polished template. The partner's team called it "very AI generated" and asked for the real drinks, ingredients, packaging and why each one suits after exercise, so they can recommend it with confidence.
**Signal:** not a no. A buyer who plans to recommend needs proof they can repeat. Polish without product photos reads as fake.
**Fix:** real drink photos, packaging panels and a tasting for whoever signs off, before any designed document (D-007, T-032).
**Rule for next time:** show the real product first. The designed pack comes second.
