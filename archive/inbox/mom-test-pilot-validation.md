## 1.0 THE DIAGNOSTIC DIRECTIVE (THE MISTAKE)

* **The Mom Test Rule:** *You cannot ask people if your business is good. They will lie to you to be nice.* You must ask about what they did in the past, not what they might do in the future. 
* **Innovation Accounting Rule (Ries):** If your vanity metrics (rapport, coffee chats, friendly nods) are high, but your validated learning and revenue are zero, you are scaling an illusion.

When engaging a high-value operator like Riad, you are legally (in the M.O.E. framework) forbidden from pitching portals, terminals, or code. You operate strictly through diagnostic questioning.

1. **The Past-Action Probe:** *"When was the last time you tried to solve your staff-hiring or client-retention bottleneck? Walk me through what you actually did, what it cost you in time, and where it failed."*
   *(If they haven't spent time or money trying to fix it, it is not a real pain point—it is a casual complaint. Do not build for it.)*
2. **The Financial Consequence Probe:** *"What is that specific operational leak currently costing your business per month in lost client lifetime value or unbooked hours?"*
   *(Force them to put a dollar figure on the friction.)*
3. **The Unscalable Proof Probe:** *"If I were to help you vet and structure a turnkey operating system for your floor starting Monday, what proof do you need to see before allocating a fixed pilot budget?"*

# VERSION B

PROTOCOL: THE MOM TEST AUDIT (HVO / PEAK VALIDATION)

Classification: S-RANK COMMERCIAL CONVERSION
Objective: Strip away software features and unscalable emotional pitches to test a studio owner's immediate operational pain and actual willingness to pay.

1.0 THE DIAGNOSTIC RULE

When pitching high-value operators (HVOs) like Riad at Peak, you do not talk about your portal, your dashboard, or your code. A studio owner cares about two things: Time and Cash Flow Friction. If they are not actively feeling an unmanageable bottleneck, any software you pitch sounds like an expensive luxury or an unnecessary distraction.

2.0 THE PRE-FLIGHT QUESTIONING MATRIX (THE 3 RULES)

You must execute these three questions before proposing any commercial retainer or portal deployment. Do not accept polite compliments ("That looks cool"). Hunt for past behavior and actual financial loss.

Rule 1: Talk about their life/operations instead of your idea.

Bad Question: "Do you think an automated booking and client diagnostic portal would be useful for your studio?"

Good Question: "How do you currently handle client onboarding and tracking when a high-value athlete drops off the floor?"

Rule 2: Ask about specifics in the past rather than generic opinions about the future.

Bad Question: "Would you pay a monthly retainer for software that saves you time?"

Good Question: "When was the last time you lost a client or a trainer because of scheduling or operational friction, and what did that cost your business?"

Rule 3: Look for real skin in the game (Action over Compliments).

The Trap: If they say, "That's an amazing system, let's stay in touch," you have failed.

The Conversion: "To fix that specific leak you just mentioned, we would need to map your station data into our terminal. If I set up the pilot by Tuesday, are you ready to run a 30-day live test with actual client data?"

3.0 THE EXECUTION MANDATE

Stop Selling Platforms: Position yourself strictly as an Infrastructure Architect solving a quantified leak.

Flip the Dynamic: If they offer a discount or position you as a consumer/client, use diagnostic questioning to turn the focus back onto their business metrics.

Secure the Pilot: Never deploy code or open access without a clear, pre-agreed behavioral commitment.
